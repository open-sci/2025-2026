The Foundation

Universities are interconnected through an invisible web of knowledge. Every citation is a link, a bridge between institutions, transferring ideas and shaping the landscape of research.

We set out to map this landscape. By analyzing citation flows between universities, we uncover patterns of influence, collaboration, and the dissemination of knowledge across Italy.

Research Question I: The Map of Italian Science

Every scientific publication is a node in a global network — it cites and is cited, connecting researchers across institutions, disciplines, and borders. But which borders? And do all Italian universities connect to the world in the same way, or does each institution carry its own geographic signature?

The Map of Italian Science traces the citation flows of six major Italian research institutions — the University of Bologna, the University of Milan, the University of Padua, the University of Turin, the University of Eastern Piedmont, and the Scuola Normale Superiore — using open bibliographic data from OpenCitations. For each institution, we mapped both directions: which countries and organizations cite their research (incoming), and which countries and organizations they cite in return (outgoing).

The result is a portrait of Italian science as a networked system — who it talks to, who talks back, and where the conversation is uneven.

The map shows the top 40 citation partners for each institution, in both directions. Use the controls to switch between institutions and between incoming and outgoing flows.

The first thing the data tells you is how similar the six institutions are. Regardless of size, location, or disciplinary focus, all six Italian universities draw from and contribute to the same core group of countries.

This is not a coincidence — it reflects the structure of global science itself, where a small number of countries concentrate the majority of research output, journal infrastructure, and citation activity.

Six countries appear in the top 12 citation partners of every institution, in both directions: the United States, France, the United Kingdom, Germany, China, and Spain.

Each bubble represents a country, sized by its average citation volume across all six institutions. Universal partners (present in all six institutions' top 12) are highlighted in gold, while others remain subtle.

Moving from countries to organizations reveals a similar structural convergence: all six Italian institutions engage with the same core set of international partners.

CNRS stands out as the single organization with the highest citation volume across all six institutions — and uniquely, its bubble sits almost exactly on the diagonal line of perfect symmetry, meaning Italian universities cite it as much as it cites them.
